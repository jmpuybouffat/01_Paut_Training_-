2. Specimen and Nozzle coordinates

The model represents a set-through branch in a main pipe. Witness geometry: main Ø400×20mm, nozzle Ø160×12mm, axis angle63°, offset0, main length800mm, external nozzle length320mm. Bevel35°, gap2mm, land2mm, nominal weld width14mm and reinforcement3mm are adjustable assumptions.

QuantityConvention
World frameRight-handed, millimetres. Origin at main-pipe centre; main axis is Z.
AzimuthAngle around Z:0° towards +X,90° towards +Y. It is not a distance in millimetres.
Z positionDistance along the main pipe; negative values are valid. An800mm pipe ends at−400/+400mm.
DepthRadial distance from outer wall inward, distinct from global Z.
Local skewTangent-plane probe heading from +Z, distinct from position azimuth and MWB refracted angle.

In the geometry view, red is the outer-surface intersection and turquoise the nominal geometric root at the main inner wall. Dashed preparation-edge curves are illustrative offsets, not measured fusion boundaries.

The nozzle is visually trimmed, not a verified Boolean CAD weld solid. Faceted root/cap profiles are repeatable assumptions. Microstructure parameters are metadata only; no anisotropic or grain-scattering calculation is performed.

3. MWB, access and flaw setup

Nozzle offers single-element MWB (45/60/70°, proposed2/4/5MHz) or 32-element5MHz PA. Actual probe/wedge reference and dimensions remain pending.PA uses the plate’s initial array parameters, with fields and projections adapted to the Nozzle cylindrical frame.

The equivalent field uses entered VT. Carbon-steel witness values are VL5920m/s, VT3240m/s and density7850kg/m³. Wedge transfer, refraction and curved contact are not solved by the scalar model.

In Training, Main-wall scan demo supplies five artificial wall targets for gesture practice, not real weld-bevel flaws. Custom specimen uses the project register. Incomplete azimuth/Z/depth/orientation prevents a flaw from generating a teaching response.

The proposed register has two6×3mm lack-of-fusion surfaces, one2mm-diameter inclusion and two4×2mm cracks; dimensions are editable. Their position and normal are scenario data, not automatic weld-face placement. The witness enables MF1 only.

The access table records angular/Z bounds, available clearance, wedge footprint, travel and margins. PLANNED means planned, not inspected. Unassessed areas allow teaching exploration, not proven real access. An exclusion without usable bounds blocks travel.

5. PA32: sector, incident field and backscatter

Tab2 Method selects PA32; tab3’s Load PA32-element/5MHz witness test supplies a repeatable case. It sets mechanical step2mm, separate from angular step2°. The array has32 elements,0.5mm pitch and12mm elevation. Element width0.45mm and30mm focus are demonstration values. Nominal55°T wedge is a starting reference; wedge propagation is not calculated.

Element phases excite the finite flaw surface, and reception uses the same coherent aperture with reciprocal phases. Surface field and A-scan come from this scalar calculation, not a relabelled MWB reflected teaching field.

  1. Load the PA witness and wait.
  2. Check Az−50°, Z−100mm, heading0°, selected45°, sector35–70° and Δθ2°.
  3. GateA0–20µs, threshold0.001, gain10dB0dB. Direct MF1 time remains near8.73µs; amplitude before gain is approximately@@PAAMP@@ at this pose.
  4. Inspect the calculated PA incident field/white ray, backscattered field and selected-angle A-scan. Change selected angle to65°, wait and compare reception; restore45°.
  5. S-scan backprojects sector responses into Z/radial depth, direct paths only. Circumferential travel may be compressed by this projection; consult3D.
  6. Record point at several poses. B-scan uses first ray35°, not the selected A-scan angle. Export scan and scene to retain sector angles/responses.

Real examination preparation supports PA settings/strategy, but no instrument is connected and no synthetic echo is presented as a real acquisition. Coverage, hardware wedge delay laws and acceptance thresholds are not qualified.

7. Interpret the operator views

Surface3D and flaw focus

Probe symbol and rays are geometric references. In computed scattering, pink points discretize the finite flaw surface and coloured points sample its outgoing field. The orange segment from probe to target centre is not an incident-field map. Focus computed flaw changes the camera, not probe position.

@@FIG:focused@@

Section and secondary3D view

Section is a Z/radial-depth projection; circumferential travel may look compressed. Nozzle3D+rays clarifies it. In computed scattering, the map passes through the selected flaw: horizontal Z, vertical radial depth, azimuth stated. It does not automatically describe the entire weld volume.

@@FIG:section@@

A-scan and gates

Horizontal: time in µs. Vertical: uncalibrated relative envelope. GateA/B/C selects interval and threshold. Current peak changes with pose; stored maximum is historical, not the current response. Gate or bounds changes reset that maximum.

In scalar mode, amplitude comes from computed reception; time is2×distance to centre/VT. The graphic pulse is not a solved broadband impulse response. In teaching mode, geometry/flaw responses use separate heuristics.

@@FIG:ascan@@

Scattered field and colour cutoff

Lobes/fringes result from complex finite-surface contributions, not extra defects. Threshold(dB) hides weak points without changing computed reception. MF1 or Strongest received selects the target whose field is displayed.

@@FIG:field@@

Historical and direct-field modes

The first model helps trace reflected paths, with illustrative amplitudes. The computed direct field contains aperture interference but no solved wall-bounced field. Changing modes is not a calibrated probe comparison.

8. Repeatable scalar witness exercise

  1. Export personal session data, then select Load backscatter witness test — MWB45°/4MHz.
  2. Check400×20/160×12mm geometry,63°, VT3240m/s. Only MF1 active:6×3mm face, Az−50°, Z−90mm, depth10mm, orientation0°, normal tilt45°.
  3. Probe starts Az−50°, Z−100mm, heading0°. Choose computed backscatter and MF1/Strongest received.
  4. Wait for completion. Old results are withdrawn and Record point disabled while pending.
  5. GateA, start0, end20µs, threshold0.01, gain10dB, field cutoff−40dB. Expected centre-path time is about8.73µs; model relative amplitude about0.062 before display gain.
  6. Focus computed flaw. Adjust heading in small increments, then compare+20° after calculation. Compare current echo, not the stored maximum.
  7. Reload witness; tab2, change MF1 normal tilt45→80°. Return to scan and compare. Reload to restore the reference.
  8. Compare4/5MHz after reloading and wait for the new calculation. Wavelength affects phase and quadrature; this is not recolouring.
  9. Change gate0–20 to30–40µs: direct echo leaves the gate, but harmonic field remains. Disable MF1: no flaw field/echo.

This checks the reduced model.8.73µs and0.062 are not instrument-calibration values or weld acceptance criteria.

Record point stores a pose, gate, current response and geometric ray references. Motion alone builds the trail, not the same characterization samples.−6dB extent uses compatible recorded samples; it is not certified flaw size.

Real examination preparation has no instrument acquisition connected; synthetic signals are not presented as real measurements. US319 reference/revision, cascade and criteria require documentation/validation. This mode does not award certification.

10. Colours, gain and differences from plate P03

Computed scattering uses fixed reference0.05 in model units,−60..0dB. Cutoff does not normalize the field. Direct Green field uses per-grid/frequency normalization: compare patterns, not calibrated absolute amplitude. The first teaching model is another representation.

Both methods now use dB gain: initially10dB for MWB,30dB for PA, range0–60dB. This matches the plate convention. Historical ×3 gain references describe earlier versions. Display gain and gates do not alter solved harmonic scattering. Colours from different modes are not interchangeable without references.

CIVA and MANTIS/GEKKO guide operator-readability review, but no exact manufacturer LUT or solver equivalence is claimed. Warm lobes mean high model amplitude, not certain flaw presence or weld rejection.

Nozzle: main axisZ, positions Az/Z; plate: weld axisX, positions X/Y. Nozzle: MWB/PA32; plate: SE/MWB/PA. Nozzle scalar scattering: direct, no wall-bounce scattering; plateP03: one opposite-face image per transmit/receive leg. Preserve these distinctions in tests and integration.

11. Fabien’s camera handoff — no headset

The goal is training controlled by PC/external camera without a headset. Nozzle currently supplies scene data and a local read API. Video detection, camera/specimen calibration and a tracked-pose input must be connected with Fabien.

const api = window.BYTE_NDT;
const pose = api.getPose();
const scene = api.getScene();
const rays = api.getBeamRays();

These functions run inside the operator document. Streamlit hosts it in an iframe; the adapter must account for that boundary. They are not video input, network protocol or hardware commands. Do not assume the plate’s tracked-pose input API already exists in Nozzle.

rotation_matrix_local_to_world is3×3 with basis vectors in columns: localX forward tangent, localY side, localZ outward normal. Right-handed, millimetres; multiply positions/dimensions by0.001 for metres and transform rotations too if the engine changes coordinates.

  1. Reproduce chapter8’s witness.
  2. Export scene; check emergence point, normals and scale. A camera marker centre need not be that emergence point.
  3. Use pose, rays and BYTE_NDT_MOTION_STOP event in Fabien’s environment.
  4. Check scalar_scattering.computation_pending and associated pose; never show an old field as a new pose’s result.
  5. Agree tracked-pose input, confidence, tracking loss, contact, stops and accuracy/latency tests.

Outgoing field points are in scalar_scattering.field_samples_world with section/events nearby. VR_SCENE_SCHEMA.md specifies them. beam.segments_world_mm are geometric references, not computed scattered-field lines. Export is not a verified weld mesh, completed camera client or WebXR session.

13. Scope and reviewer feedback

Figures come from the actual local Streamlit application in both languages. Software checks cover witness, exports, languages, gestures and manual downloads, not industrial qualification.

Scattering is homogeneous, harmonic scalar, direct-illumination and single-scattering. No incident/scattered wall bounces, coupled elastic modes, wedge, curved contact, multiple interactions, GTD or full weld/branch propagation. Inclusion is an approximate reflecting sphere, not a real inclusion-contrast solver.

Useful feedback states Nozzle REV0.5 SCALAR BACKSCATTER, language, field mode, geometry, MWB/frequency, azimuth/Z/skew, flaw/normal, gate, gain, action, observed/expected result, screenshot and export. Separate gesture, pedagogy and physics comments.
